Engineering Questions
• Which model achieved the best accuracy?
The ResNet18 fine-tuned last block achieved the best accuracy with a test accuracy of 1.0000.

• Which model trained fastest?
The SimpleCNN from scratch trained the fastest with a training time of 0.79 seconds.

• Which model required the least implementation effort?
The ResNet18 frozen feature extractor required the least implementation effort, as it only involved replacing the final linear layer and training a new classifier head, without needing to fine-tune any of the convolutional layers.

• Which model is most suitable for a small dataset?
The ResNet18 frozen feature extractor is most suitable for a small dataset, as it leverages pretrained features from ImageNet, which can help improve performance even when the amount of training data is limited. The SimpleCNN may struggle to learn effective features from a small dataset, while the fine-tuned ResNet18 may require more data to avoid overfitting when updating the convolutional layers.

• Which model is most suitable for a real operational system?
The ResNet18 frozen feature extractor is most suitable for a real operational system, as it provides a good balance of high accuracy and reasonable training time. It can be deployed more quickly than the fine-tuned version while still achieving strong performance, making it a practical choice for real-world applications where resources and time may be limited.

• When would you train a CNN from scratch?
When you don't have access to a suitable pretrained model, or when your dataset is significantly different from the datasets used for pretraining (e.g., medical images, satellite images) and you have enough data to train a model effectively from scratch. Or when you don't have enought computational resources to fine-tune a large pretrained model.

• When would you use transfer learning?
When you have a limited amount of training data and want to leverage the knowledge learned from a large dataset (like ImageNet) to improve performance on your specific task. Transfer learning can help achieve better accuracy with less training time, especially when the target task is similar to the pretraining task.

• When would you fine-tune a pretrained model?
When you have a moderate amount of training data and want to achieve the best possible performance by allowing the model to adapt the pretrained features to your specific task. Fine-tuning can be particularly beneficial when the target task is somewhat different from the pretraining task, as it allows the model to learn more task-specific features while still benefiting from the general visual features learned during pretraining.
